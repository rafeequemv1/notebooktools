Step by step

10 ways to study smarter this week

01Study guide · PDF

Turn lecture notes into a study guide

The job: Weeks of slides and notes, and the exam is on Friday.

  1. Make a notebook for the subject and add your lecture slides, notes and textbook chapters.
  2. Paste the prompt below and name the topic.
  3. In the ribbon, click Export, then Last answer only, then PDF, and keep it with your notes.
Prompt to paste
From my sources, make a study guide for [subject/topic]. Include: the key concepts with one-line definitions, the main ideas in a logical order, two worked examples, common mistakes students make, and 10 practice questions with answers in a separate section at the end.

02Flashcards & quiz

Flashcards, quizzes and a mind map

The job: Rereading feels like studying. Testing yourself actually is.

  1. Open Studio and generate Flashcards and a Quiz from the chapter.
  2. Make a Mind map to see how the topics connect.
  3. Come back to the quiz a few days later and see what stuck.
Studio & podcasts
03Lecture notes · PDF

Turn a lecture playlist into notes

The job: Half your course is on YouTube, and nobody can rewatch 20 hours.

  1. Open the playlist and import every video in one click.
  2. Paste the prompt to get notes for each lecture.
  3. Export the notes as a PDF to revise from.
Prompt to paste
For each video in my sources, write revision notes: a 5-point summary, the key terms with definitions, and 3 questions I should be able to answer after watching. Title each part with the video's title.

04Practice paper · PDF

Practise with past papers

The job: You know the content but not how the exam asks about it.

  1. Add past papers and the syllabus to the notebook.
  2. Paste the prompt to find the patterns and get new practice questions.
  3. Answer them on paper, then check against the answers section.
Prompt to paste
Using the past papers in my sources, list the topics that come up most often and how they are usually asked. Then write 5 new practice questions per topic in the same style and difficulty, with model answers in a separate section at the end.
Answer to PDF / WordPrompt library
05Revision sheet · PDF

Make a one-page revision sheet

The job: The night before, you need everything on a single page.

  1. Pick one topic or chapter.
  2. Paste the prompt for a dense, one-page summary.
  3. Export as PDF and print it.
Prompt to paste
Summarise [topic] from my sources as a one-page revision sheet: the key definitions or formulas, the 5 facts I must know, 3 common exam traps, and a quick self-test of 5 short questions. Keep it short enough to fit on one page.
Answer to PDF / Word
06Explanation

Get unstuck on a hard concept

The job: The textbook explanation just isn't landing.

  1. Ask about the concept with the prompt below.
  2. Click the citation to read the exact page it comes from.
  3. Ask a follow-up until you could explain it to a friend.
Prompt to paste
Explain [concept] from my sources as if I'm new to it. Start with a simple everyday analogy, then give the proper definition, then one worked example step by step. Tell me which source and page it comes from.
Prompt library
07Audio Overview

A revision podcast for your commute

The job: Dead time on the bus could be revision time.

  1. In Studio, open Audio Overview, choose Customize and paste the focus below.
  2. Download it from Studio files in the ribbon.
  3. Listen on the way to class or the exam.
Prompt to paste
Explain [chapter or topic] for a student revising for an exam. Focus on [the parts I find hardest]. Use simple examples, and finish by asking three questions I should be able to answer.
Studio & podcasts
08Essay outline

Research an essay with real citations

The job: You need arguments you can back up, not made-up references.

  1. Add your readings, plus articles and pages from the web.
  2. Paste the prompt for arguments with their sources and an outline.
  3. Write the essay yourself, checking each claim against its citation.
Prompt to paste
From my sources, list the main arguments for and against [thesis], each with the source and page it comes from. Then suggest an outline for a [1,500]-word essay. Do not write the essay.

Bring your AI study chats in

The job: You worked through a topic in ChatGPT, but it's not in your notes.

  1. Open the conversation and click Import.
  2. The chat lands in your notebook as a source.
  3. Ask Gemini Notebook to check it against your textbook and flag anything that disagrees.

10

One notebook per subject, shared with your group

The job: Notes for six subjects end up in one messy notebook.

  1. Make one notebook per subject and put them in a folder per semester.
  2. Tag notebooks by exam date so you can filter what's next.
  3. Share a notebook with your study group so everyone works from the same sources.

Questions

Is NotebookLM good for studying?

Yes. It answers only from the material you add, such as your lecture slides and textbook, and cites the exact passage, so you can check everything. Its Studio also makes flashcards, quizzes, mind maps and Audio Overviews from the same material.

Is it free for students?

Yes. Gemini Notebook (NotebookLM) is free with a Google account, with up to 100 notebooks of 50 sources each. NotebookTools is a free Chrome extension; every feature works on the free plan.

Is using it cheating?

Using it to understand, practise and revise is studying. Follow your school's rules on AI, and write graded work yourself. The prompts on this page help you learn and plan; the essay prompt deliberately stops at an outline.

Can I study with friends?

Yes. Share a notebook with your study group so everyone asks questions of the same sources and uses the same flashcards and quizzes.
