## Add any website to NotebookLM in one click

The NotebookTools popup adds the page in your current tab to a NotebookLM notebook as a web source. It works on articles, documentation, blog posts, news and research pages, and you never leave the page or copy the URL.

### How to use it

1. Open the article or page you want to keep.
2. Click the NotebookTools icon in the Chrome toolbar. The popup shows the current tab, ready to add.
3. Pick a notebook, or type a name to create a new one. Signed in to NotebookLM with more than one Google account? Choose which one under **NotebookLM account**.
4. Click **Add page to notebook**. The page is added as a source. With Pro, **Also create in Studio** makes a mind map, report or other Studio output in the same step.

### Why it helps

Adding a URL in NotebookLM means switching tabs, opening the add-source dialog, choosing the website option and pasting the link. With research spread across many tabs, that context switch adds up. From the popup it is one click while you read. For many tabs at once, [Add open tabs](https://www.notebooktools.com/features/bulk-import) adds them all, and to keep just one passage you can [send highlighted text](https://www.notebooktools.com/features/highlights) instead. The source keeps the page link, so NotebookLM's citations lead you back to the original article whenever you need to check the context.

## Add webpage: frequently asked questions

### Can NotebookLM read any website?

NotebookLM imports the text of public web pages. Pages behind a login or paywall, and pages that load their text with scripts, may import with little or no text.

### How many websites can I add to one notebook?

Each web page counts as one source. A free Google account allows 50 sources per notebook, Google AI Plus 100 and Google AI Pro 300.

### Is adding webpages to NotebookLM free?

Yes. Every import option in NotebookTools is free, including webpages, YouTube, X posts and highlights.
