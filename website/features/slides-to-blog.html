Wikipedia

Retrieval-augmented generation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a technique that grants generative models the ability to retrieve and incorporate new information before responding.

Instead of relying only on what a model learned in training, RAG first searches a set of documents, then passes the most relevant passages to the model as context.

The approach reduces hallucinations and lets answers cite their sources, which is why research tools such as NotebookLM ground every answer in your uploads.

Harvard Business Review

Why shared sources beat smart summaries

By Priya Raman · 8 min read

Most teams now ask an AI to summarise the reading. It is fast, and it feels thorough.

Teams that review sources together make better decisions than teams that rely on summaries alone. In our study of 140 product teams, groups that read the underlying documents caught twice as many risks.

The fix is not to abandon summaries, but to keep the sources one click away — and to save the passages that matter.
