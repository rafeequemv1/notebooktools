NotebookLM Video Overviews: how to make and customize one (2026)

Updated November 19, 2026 · 8 min read

Short answer

A NotebookLM Video Overview is a narrated video, built from your sources, that explains a topic with visuals such as slides, images and diagrams. You make one from the Studio panel. Google's help lists three formats (Explainer, Short and Cinematic) plus options for visual style, language and a focus topic. It counts toward the chat and Studio usage limits that refresh every few hours.

NotebookLM (now Gemini Notebook) started with Audio Overviews: two AI hosts talking through your sources like a podcast. Video Overviews add pictures. NotebookLM writes a script from your sources, narrates it, and pairs it with visuals. The result is a short explainer you can watch, download or share.

What Video Overviews are

A Video Overview is one of the Studio outputs, alongside Audio Overviews, slide decks, mind maps, reports, study guides, flashcards and quizzes. Like the others, it is grounded in the sources you select in the notebook. It is useful when a topic is easier to understand with a diagram, a timeline or a quote on screen than with audio alone.

The video is AI-generated. Narration, visuals and facts can contain mistakes, so check anything important against the cited sources in chat.

How to make a video in NotebookLM

  1. Open a notebook at notebooklm.google.com and add your sources: PDFs, Google Docs or Slides, web pages, YouTube videos, audio files or pasted text.
  2. Select the sources to cover. Tick only the sources the video should use in the Sources panel.
  3. Open the Video Overview options in the Studio panel. Use the edit (pencil) option next to Video Overview to customize it, or select Video Overview directly to use the defaults.
  4. Choose your settings: format, visual style, language and what the video should focus on (see the next section).
  5. Generate. Videos take longer than most Studio outputs. You can keep working in the notebook while it renders.
  6. Watch, then download or share. The finished video appears in the Studio panel.

Writing a good focus prompt

The focus box is where you get the most control. Treat it like a brief to a video editor: who is watching, what they should learn, and what to leave out.

  • "Explain this to first-year students with no background. Cover the three main causes and end with a one-sentence summary."
  • "Focus only on chapter 4. Use a timeline to show the order of events."
  • "For a team meeting: summarize the key findings and the open questions. Skip the methodology."
  • "Compare the two approaches in these papers side by side, with their trade-offs."

Many of the same ideas work for slide decks. Our NotebookLM slide prompts guide has templates you can adapt, and the NotebookLM slide styles gallery shows how different visual directions look.

Usage limits

Since September 2026, NotebookLM measures chat and Studio use with limits that refresh every few hours, up to a weekly cap. Video Overviews draw on the same allowance as other Studio outputs, and they are among the heavier ones. Paid Google AI plans get more. Google does not publish a fixed number of videos per day for every plan, so if you hit the limit, wait for it to refresh or check the plan page inside Gemini Notebook.

Source limits also apply: 50 sources per notebook on the free plan, 100 on Google AI Plus and 300 on Google AI Pro, with each source up to 500,000 words or 200 MB.

Downloading and sharing

  • Download. Google's help describes a Download option for the video, so you can share the file itself.
  • Share a link. Viewers need access to the notebook. Share the notebook with them or make it public. Google notes that public sharing is not available for Workspace Enterprise or Education accounts.
  • Deleting breaks links. If you delete a Video Overview, links you shared to it stop working.

Many people make both from the same sources: a video to understand the topic once, and an audio version to review it later. The NotebookTools Studio tools collect Audio and Video Overviews from every notebook in one place, so you are not hunting through notebooks to find last week's video.

Tips for better Video Overviews

  • Fewer, better sources. A video covers a lot in a few minutes. Select the sources that matter and leave the rest unticked.
  • One idea per video. Make three short videos on three subtopics rather than one long video on everything.
  • Name the audience. "For a ten-year-old" and "for a board meeting" give very different scripts.
  • Use producer notes. When available, review the plan before rendering. It is cheaper to fix the outline than to regenerate a video.
  • Check facts in chat. Ask the chat about any claim you plan to repeat; the answer comes with citations.
  • Pair with a mind map. A NotebookLM mind map shows the structure; the video explains it.

FAQ

How do I make a video in NotebookLM?

Open a notebook, select your sources, and choose Video Overview in the Studio panel. Use the edit option first if you want to set the format, visual style, language or focus. The video appears in Studio when it finishes.

What formats do NotebookLM Video Overviews have?

Google's help lists Explainer, Short and Cinematic. Cinematic is listed as 18+ and English only.

Can I choose the visual style of a NotebookLM video?

Yes, for Explainer videos. Google lists presets such as Classic, Whiteboard and Anime, an automatic option, and a Custom style you describe yourself (18+).

How many Video Overviews can I make?

Google does not publish a fixed daily number for every plan. Since September 2026, chat and Studio use is measured with limits that refresh every few hours, up to a weekly cap, and paid plans get more.

Can I download a NotebookLM Video Overview?

Yes. Google's help describes a Download option so you can share the video file. You can also share a link, but viewers need access to the notebook.

What is the difference between Video and Audio Overviews?

Video Overviews add narrated visuals and come in Explainer, Short and Cinematic formats. Audio Overviews are podcast-style and come in Deep Dive, Brief, Critique and Debate formats.
