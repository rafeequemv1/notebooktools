September 2026: rolling usage limits

Since September 2026, chat and Studio use (Audio Overviews, Video Overviews, slide decks, reports and the rest) is measured with limits that refresh every few hours, up to a weekly cap. Free accounts get a base allowance; Google AI Plus, Pro and Ultra get more.

In practice:

  • If you hit a limit mid-session, wait a few hours and it refreshes.
  • Heavy weeks can reach the weekly cap; it resets later in the week.
  • Source limits did not change: they still depend on your plan, and each source can be up to 500,000 words or 200 MB.

If you see a limit message, our NotebookLM troubleshooting guide explains the options.

2026: NotebookLM becomes Gemini Notebook

Google renamed NotebookLM (now Gemini Notebook) in 2026. It is the same product, existing notebooks carried over, and it is still at notebooklm.google.com. Read What is Gemini Notebook? for what the rename does and does not change, and NotebookLM vs Gemini for how it differs from the Gemini app.

Earlier feature timeline

Major features with dates confirmed by Google announcements or wide press coverage:

  1. November 2025: Flashcards and quizzes arrived in the NotebookLM mobile app.
  2. September 8, 2025: Flashcards, quizzes and new report formats began rolling out on the web.
  3. July 29, 2025: Video Overviews launched (narrated slides, English first), along with a redesigned Studio panel that lets you create several outputs of the same type in one notebook.
  4. September 11, 2024: Audio Overviews launched, turning sources into a podcast-style discussion between two AI hosts.

The pattern is clear: NotebookLM started as a question-and-answer tool for your documents, then added ways to turn the same sources into something to listen to, watch or study from. Most 2025 and 2026 additions live in the Studio panel, which is also where the September 2026 usage limits matter most.

How to adapt to the 2026 changes

The rename changes nothing in your workflow. The usage limits can. Five habits help on any plan:

  1. Plan Studio outputs. Decide what you need (an Audio Overview, a study guide, a slide deck) before generating, instead of regenerating the same output many times.
  2. Ask focused questions. One clear question per message gets better answers and wastes less of your allowance.
  3. Save answers as notes. A saved note does not need to be asked again next week.
  4. Keep notebooks lean. Delete duplicate and stale sources so answers stay relevant and you stay under the source limit.
  5. Spread heavy work across the week. Because limits refresh every few hours, several short sessions go further than one long one before a deadline.

If you regularly hit the weekly cap, compare what Google AI Plus and Pro add in our pricing guide before upgrading. Many students and researchers stay on the free plan by splitting projects across notebooks.

What did not change

  • The address: notebooklm.google.com.
  • Your existing notebooks, sources, notes and Studio outputs.
  • The free plan: 100 notebooks and 50 sources per notebook with any personal Google account.
  • The per-source size cap: 500,000 words or 200 MB on every plan.
  • The supported source types: PDFs, Google Docs, Slides and Sheets, web URLs, YouTube videos, audio files and pasted text.

How to check for NotebookLM changes yourself

  1. Read Google’s announcements. New NotebookLM features are announced on Google’s blog and, for Workspace accounts, on the Google Workspace Updates blog.
  2. Check the status dashboard. If something suddenly stops working, the Google Workspace Status Dashboard shows whether NotebookLM has an open incident.
  3. Look at your plan page. The plan and limit details inside Gemini Notebook are the most current numbers for your account.
  4. Come back here monthly. We summarize the changes that matter and link to the guides we update.
