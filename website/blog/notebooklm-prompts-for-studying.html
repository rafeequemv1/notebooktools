General study prompts (1–10)

1. Study guide

Prompt

Create a study guide from my sources. Include: a one-paragraph overview, the 10 most important concepts with plain-English explanations, key terms in bold, and 10 review questions with answers at the end.

When to use: The first thing to run on a new course notebook. This is the go-to NotebookLM study guide prompt.

2. Explain it simply

Prompt

Explain [CONCEPT] as if I were new to the subject. Use one everyday analogy, then give the precise definition from my sources, then one worked example.

When to use: When a lecture or chapter lost you. Replace [CONCEPT] with the topic.

3. Key terms glossary

Prompt

Build a glossary of every technical term in my sources. For each: a one-sentence definition, which source it comes from, and one example. Sort alphabetically.

When to use: Vocabulary-heavy courses. Paste the result into your flashcard app.

4. Chapter outline

Prompt

Make a hierarchical outline of [CHAPTER OR LECTURE]. Use main topics, subtopics, and bullet points for facts I need to memorize. Keep the order logical, not the order of the source.

When to use: Turning a long reading into something you can review in five minutes.

5. What matters most

Prompt

If I only had one hour to study these sources, what 8 ideas should I learn first, and why? Rank them by how often they come up across the sources.

When to use: Short on time. Helps you prioritize instead of re-reading everything.

6. Connect the sources

Prompt

How do the lecture notes, the textbook chapter and the slides relate to each other on [TOPIC]? Point out where they agree, where one adds detail, and where they differ.

When to use: Notebooks that mix lectures, readings and slides.

7. Common misconceptions

Prompt

List the 6 most common misunderstandings students have about [TOPIC], based on my sources. For each, explain the correct idea and cite the source.

When to use: Before a test, to catch the traps examiners like.

8. Feynman check

Prompt

I will explain [CONCEPT] in my own words. Tell me what I got right, what is missing, and what is wrong, citing my sources. My explanation: [PASTE YOUR EXPLANATION]

When to use: Testing whether you really understand something, not just recognize it.

9. Summary sheet

Prompt

Write a one-page summary sheet of my sources: key formulas, definitions, dates or rules, in compact bullet points I can review the night before.

When to use: Making a cheat sheet-style review page (for revision, not for the exam room).

10. Weekly review

Prompt

Summarize what I added to this notebook this week: [LIST THE NEW SOURCES]. Then list 5 questions I should be able to answer now.

When to use: Keeping up during the term instead of cramming at the end.

Exam prep prompts (11–16)

11. Practice exam

Prompt

Write a 20-question practice exam from my sources only: 10 multiple choice with explanations, 6 short answer, 4 longer questions. Put the answer key at the end.

When to use: A week before the exam. Answer on paper first, then check.

12. Past paper style

Prompt

Here is a past exam question: [PASTE QUESTION]. Write 5 new questions in the same style and difficulty, using only my sources, with model answers.

When to use: When you have past papers and want more of the same.

13. Grade my answer

Prompt

Grade my answer to this question as a strict examiner. Give a mark out of 10, list what is missing, and show a model answer using my sources. Question: [QUESTION] My answer: [ANSWER]

When to use: Essay and short-answer subjects. Repeat until your answer scores well.

14. Weak spots

Prompt

Quiz me on [TOPIC] one question at a time. Wait for my answer before giving the next question. After 10 questions, tell me which subtopics I got wrong most.

When to use: Active recall in the chat. Works best in a focused notebook.

15. Revision plan

Prompt

My exam is in [NUMBER] days and I can study [HOURS] hours a day. Using my sources, make a day-by-day revision plan that covers every topic and leaves the last day for review.

When to use: Planning the run-up to an exam.

16. Last-minute review

Prompt

Give me the 15 facts from my sources most likely to appear on the exam, each in one sentence, with the source it comes from.

When to use: The morning of the exam.

NotebookLM prompts for nursing students (17–19)

Add your pharmacology notes, care plan templates and clinical guidelines as sources first. NotebookLM only knows what is in the notebook.

17. Drug cards

Prompt

For each medication in my sources, make a card with: drug class, mechanism of action, main indications, key side effects, contraindications and nursing considerations. Use only my sources.

When to use: Pharmacology. Check every card against your course drug guide.

18. Care plan practice

Prompt

Using my sources, build a nursing care plan for a patient with [CONDITION]: assessment findings, nursing diagnoses, goals, interventions with rationales, and evaluation.

When to use: Care plan assignments and clinical prep.

19. NCLEX-style questions

Prompt

Write 10 NCLEX-style questions on [TOPIC] from my sources, including prioritization and select-all-that-apply items. Explain why each wrong option is wrong.

When to use: Licensing exam prep. These are the most-asked-for NotebookLM prompts for nursing students.

Prompts for medical students (20–22)

20. Clinical case

Prompt

Write a clinical case for [CONDITION] based on my sources: presentation, history, exam findings and investigations. Then ask me for the diagnosis and management before showing the answer.

When to use: Practising clinical reasoning from lecture material.

21. Differential diagnosis table

Prompt

Make a table of the differential diagnoses for [SYMPTOM] covered in my sources, with columns: condition, distinguishing features, key investigation, first-line treatment.

When to use: Comparing look-alike conditions.

22. Mechanism chain

Prompt

Explain the pathophysiology of [CONDITION] as a numbered chain of cause and effect, from the first change to the clinical signs. Cite the source for each step.

When to use: Understanding why, not just memorizing what.

These prompts are for studying. Always confirm clinical facts against your course materials and official guidelines.

Prompts for law students (23–24)

23. Case brief

Prompt

Brief [CASE NAME] from my sources: facts, issue, holding, reasoning, and why it matters for [TOPIC]. Keep it under 300 words.

When to use: Case reading for seminars. Add the judgment or casebook extract as a source first.

24. Issue spotting

Prompt

Here is a problem question: [PASTE]. Identify every legal issue it raises, the rule from my sources for each, and how the rule applies to these facts.

When to use: Problem-question exams (IRAC practice).

Prompts for engineering and maths (25–26)

25. Worked examples

Prompt

Take the method for [TOPIC] from my sources and show it step by step on a new example. Then give me 3 similar problems to solve, with answers hidden at the end.

When to use: Maths, physics and engineering problem sets.

26. Formula sheet

Prompt

List every formula in my sources on [TOPIC]. For each: what each symbol means, the units, when to use it, and one common mistake.

When to use: Before a problem-based exam. Double-check formulas against the lecture slides.

NotebookLM is strongest at explaining methods from your material. Check every calculation yourself; do not trust arithmetic without working it through.

Prompts for language learning (27–28)

27. Vocabulary in context

Prompt

From my sources, list 25 useful words and phrases in [LANGUAGE] with a translation, the sentence where each appears, and one new example sentence.

When to use: Learning vocabulary from readings, transcripts or YouTube lessons.

28. Grammar drill

Prompt

Explain the grammar rule for [RULE] in [LANGUAGE] using examples from my sources. Then give me 10 fill-in-the-blank sentences to practise, with answers at the end.

When to use: Drilling one grammar point at a time.

Prompts for history and humanities (29–30)

29. Timeline

Prompt

Build a timeline of [PERIOD OR TOPIC] from my sources: date, event, key people, and one line on why it mattered. Flag any dates where sources disagree.

When to use: Getting the sequence straight before an essay.

30. Essay argument

Prompt

Help me plan an essay on: [QUESTION]. Give a thesis, three arguments with evidence from my sources, one counter-argument, and how to answer it.

When to use: Essay-based history and humanities exams.

Tips for better study answers

  • Say “from my sources only” when you want answers limited to course material.
  • Select fewer sources. Untick sources in the panel to focus a prompt on one lecture.
  • Ask for a format. Tables, numbered steps and word limits make answers easier to revise from.
  • Follow up. “Go deeper on point 3” usually beats starting over.
  • Keep prompts handy. The NotebookLM prompt library in NotebookTools Pro lets you insert saved prompts by typing / in the chat. More study tools are on our page for students.

FAQ

What is the best NotebookLM prompt for studying?

Start with a study guide prompt: ask for an overview, the 10 most important concepts, key terms and review questions with answers, using only your sources. Then switch to practice questions for active recall.

What is a good NotebookLM study guide prompt?

Try: “Create a study guide from my sources. Include a one-paragraph overview, the 10 most important concepts with plain-English explanations, key terms in bold, and 10 review questions with answers.” Studio also has a built-in study guide output.

Are there NotebookLM prompts for nursing students?

Yes. Useful ones turn pharmacology notes into drug cards, build practice care plans, and write NCLEX-style questions with explanations. Check every answer against your course materials.

Can NotebookLM make practice exams and flashcards?

Yes. You can prompt for a practice exam in the chat, and Studio can generate flashcards and quizzes from your sources.

Does NotebookLM use information outside my sources?

Answers are grounded in the sources in your notebook and include citations. Adding “from my sources only” to a prompt keeps the focus on your material.
