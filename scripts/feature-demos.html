Send YouTube to NotebookLM in one click

NotebookTools puts a NotebookLM button on every YouTube watch page, next to Like and Share. Click it, pick a notebook, and the video is added as a source in NotebookLM (now Gemini Notebook), ready to chat with, summarize or turn into an Audio Overview. For a whole playlist or channel, the side panel loads every video at once.

How to use it

  1. Install NotebookTools from the Chrome Web Store and sign in to NotebookLM in the same Chrome profile.
  2. Open a YouTube video and click the NotebookLM button under the player.
  3. Search your notebooks and pick one, or type a name to create a new notebook.
  4. Click Import. The video appears in the notebook's sources, and you can open the notebook from the panel.
  5. For a playlist or channel, open its page, then click Load from tab in the side panel's Bulk tab, tick the videos and import them.

Why it helps

Without the extension you copy each link, switch to NotebookLM, open the add-source dialog and paste it. That is fine for one video and slow for a 30-lecture course. The playlist loader skips videos already in the queue, and when a playlist has more than 50 videos it splits them across notebooks, so you stay within the free per-notebook source limit.

Free. Importing single videos, playlists and channels is part of the free plan. Creating Studio outputs straight from YouTube is part of Pro ($49 one-time, lifetime). See plans

Retrieval-augmented generation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a technique that grants generative models the ability to retrieve and incorporate new information before responding.

Instead of relying only on what a model learned in training, RAG first searches a set of documents, then passes the most relevant passages to the model as context.

The approach reduces hallucinations and lets answers cite their sources, which is why research tools such as NotebookLM ground every answer in your uploads.

Bulk import to NotebookLM: dozens of sources in one run

The Bulk tab in the NotebookTools side panel builds a queue of links and imports them into one NotebookLM notebook in a single run. Add your open tabs, paste a list of URLs, extract the links from an article, or load a YouTube playlist or channel. Each item shows its type and status, and failed items can be retried.

How to use it

  1. Click the NotebookTools icon in the Chrome toolbar and open the Bulk tab.
  2. Build the queue with Open tabs, Paste links (one per line), Extract from page, or Load from tab on a YouTube playlist.
  3. Untick anything you don't want, or select items and click Remove.
  4. Pick an existing notebook or create a new one.
  5. Click Import queue and watch the progress bar until the summary appears.

Why it helps

Adding sources by hand means one dialog per link, which is a lot of clicking for a reading list of 40 articles. The queue imports them in one go, skips links that are already in it, and warns you when it would push a notebook past its source limit (50 sources on a free Google account, 300 on Google AI Pro). You can also right-click any page and choose Extract links to Notebook to queue every link in it.

Manage NotebookLM sources and delete duplicate sources

Open any notebook in the NotebookTools hub and the Sources tab lists every source with its type. From there you can select many sources at once, move them into folders, delete them, and scan the notebook for duplicate sources so each slot holds something new.

How to use it

  1. Open the notebooks hub and click a notebook. The Sources tab lists every source.
  2. Click Manage, tick the sources you want, then organize them into folders or delete them.
  3. To find duplicates, run a fast scan (matching URLs and titles) or a deep scan (comparing content).
  4. Review each duplicate group, choose the copy to keep and remove the rest.

Why it helps

Duplicates creep in when you import the same article twice or re-add a video from a playlist. Each copy uses one of your 50 source slots on a free Google account. Deleting sources one by one in NotebookLM means a menu per source; bulk selection clears dozens in a few clicks. Folders also work for Studio files, so a notebook with 100 or more items stays easy to read.

Manage sources: frequently asked questions

How do I delete multiple sources in NotebookLM?

Open the notebook in the NotebookTools hub, click Manage on the Sources tab, tick the sources and delete them together. The deletion applies to the notebook in NotebookLM.

How does the duplicate source scan work?

The fast scan groups sources with the same URL or title. The deep scan also compares the text of each source, so it finds copies saved under different names. You choose which copy to keep in each group.

Do source folders change my notebook in NotebookLM?

No. Folders only change how NotebookTools groups your sources and Studio files. Deleting a source does remove it from NotebookLM, so review before you confirm.

Sync Google Drive sources in NotebookLM when files change

When you add a Google Doc, Slides deck, Sheet or Drive PDF to NotebookLM, the notebook works from the version you imported. NotebookTools checks those sources, plus web and YouTube sources, and marks the ones that changed with Update available, so you can re-sync them all at once.

How to use it

  1. Open a notebook in the NotebookTools hub.
  2. Wait a moment while it checks each source against the latest version.
  3. Look for the Update available label on changed sources.
  4. Click Refresh stale (N) to re-sync every changed source one by one.
  5. Or use the ↻ button to refresh a single source.

Why it helps

NotebookLM answers from the copy it holds, so an old version of your lecture notes, spec or price sheet gives old answers. Checking each Drive source by hand is slow when a notebook holds 20 of them, and it is easy to miss one. NotebookTools shows exactly which sources are stale and refreshes them with one button. That matters most for living documents: a team handbook, a course syllabus that changes each week, or a research log you keep adding to while the notebook is in use.

Teams that review sources together make better decisions than teams that rely on summaries alone. In our study of 140 product teams, groups that read the underlying documents caught twice as many risks.

The fix is not to abandon summaries, but to keep the sources one click away — and to save the passages that matter.

Save highlighted text to NotebookLM from any page

Select a passage on any web page, right-click and choose Add to NotebookLM. NotebookTools opens the side panel with your selection, you pick a notebook, and the text is saved as a source titled with the page name.

How to use it

  1. Highlight the passage you want to keep on any page.
  2. Right-click and choose Add to NotebookLM. The side panel opens with your selection.
  3. Pick the notebook the quote should go to.
  4. Click Import text. It is saved as a text source titled with the page name.

Why it helps

Adding a whole page brings in menus, comments and sidebars along with the paragraph you cared about. A highlight keeps only the quote, so NotebookLM's answers and citations point at exactly that text. Because the text comes from the page you already have open, it also works on pages NotebookLM can't fetch from a link, such as ones you are signed in to. Collect quotes from several articles into one notebook, then ask NotebookLM to compare them, build a reading summary, or turn them into flashcards for review.

Export NotebookLM chat to PDF, Markdown and more

NotebookTools adds an Export chat button above the NotebookLM chat box. It saves the whole conversation as Markdown, plain text, JSON, CSV, HTML or PDF, with the file named after your notebook. You can also export just the last answer as a print-ready PDF, a Word file or Markdown.

How to use it

  1. Open a notebook and its chat in NotebookLM.
  2. Click Export chat above the chat box.
  3. Choose Markdown, Plain text, JSON, CSV, HTML or PDF.
  4. For PDF, a print-ready page opens: choose Save as PDF in Chrome's print dialog. Other formats download straight away.

Why it helps

A good NotebookLM session is a research trail: the questions you asked and the cited answers you got. Exporting keeps a copy you can share, file with a project or paste into a report. Markdown suits note apps, CSV and JSON suit spreadsheets and scripts, and PDF suits handouts and records. The single-answer export keeps headings, lists, tables and bold text, which helps when the answer is a worksheet, exam paper or lesson plan you want to print. Citation chips and buttons are left out, so the file reads like a normal document.

Turn NotebookLM slides into a blog post with every slide image

Slides to blog takes a slide deck made in NotebookLM Studio and turns it into a written blog post or guide. The Auto blog workflow imports the deck PDF, has NotebookLM write the article, and extracts every slide as an image placed in the post.

How to use it

  1. Create a slide deck in the notebook's Studio panel. Any ready deck works.
  2. Click the orange Blog workflow button above the chat box.
  3. Run Auto blog and wait while it imports the deck, writes the article and extracts the slide images.
  4. Review the post, then download it as HTML, CSV, DOC or PDF.

Why it helps

A slide deck is good for presenting but hard to search, and it says little to readers who weren't in the room. Writing the article by hand and exporting each slide as an image is slow. The workflow does both in the notebook you already have, so the text is grounded in the same sources as the slides. Pair it with a NotebookLM slide style to get a deck worth publishing. The same notebook can then produce a blog post, a handout for a class and a guide for a client from one set of sources.

Send a screenshot to NotebookLM and get a written guide

Snapshots captures any area of your screen with Alt+Shift+S, lets you annotate it, and sends the set to a NotebookLM notebook. NotebookLM then writes a blog post or a step-by-step web app tutorial with your images placed inline.

How to use it

  1. Press Alt+Shift+S or click Drag to capture, then drag over any area of the page.
  2. Annotate with boxes, circles, arrows or the pencil, then click Save.
  3. Review your snapshots in the side panel and add a caption or notes to each one.
  4. Choose Create blog with images or Create web app tutorial; for a tutorial, add a title and a short description.
  5. Send the set to a notebook and let NotebookLM write it up.

Why it helps

Writing documentation usually means taking screenshots, cropping them and then describing each step by hand. Snapshots keeps the image, your annotation and the action you recorded together, so NotebookLM has what it needs to describe each step. The result is a how-to guide or blog post with every screenshot in the right place, ready to edit. It suits support articles, onboarding docs and internal how-tos where the screenshots carry most of the meaning.
