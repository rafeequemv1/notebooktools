Send YouTube to NotebookLM in one click

NotebookTools puts a Send to NotebookLM button on every YouTube watch page, next to Like and Share. Click it, pick a notebook, and the video is added as a source in NotebookLM (now Gemini Notebook), ready to chat with, summarize or turn into an Audio Overview. For a whole playlist or channel, the NotebookTools popup lists every video so you can add them at once.

How to use it

  1. Install NotebookTools from the Chrome Web Store and sign in to NotebookLM in the same Chrome profile.
  2. Open a YouTube video and click Send to NotebookLM under the player.
  3. Search your notebooks and pick one, or type a name to create a new notebook. With several Google accounts, the NotebookTools popup lets you choose which NotebookLM account to use.

  5. For a playlist or channel, open its page and click the NotebookTools icon in the toolbar: the popup lists the videos. Tick the ones you want and click Import N selected videos.

Why it helps

Without the extension you copy each link, switch to NotebookLM, open the add-source dialog and paste it. That is fine for one video and slow for a 30-lecture course. The playlist loader skips videos already in the queue, and when a playlist has more than 50 videos it splits them across notebooks, so you stay within the free per-notebook source limit.

Send ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and other AI chats to NotebookLM

NotebookTools adds a Send to NotebookLM button to AI chats and social posts. One click saves the whole conversation, with who said what, to a NotebookLM notebook as a clean text source. It works on ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity (with its list of sources), Grok and Kimi, and on Reddit threads, LinkedIn posts and articles, X posts and Google Docs.

How to use it

  1. Open a conversation in ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, Grok or Kimi. Shared chat links work too.
  2. Click Send to NotebookLM: in the chat's top bar on ChatGPT and Gemini, at the top right on the others.
  3. Check the title and the number of messages in the panel.

Why it helps

Good answers end up scattered across several AI apps, and none of them can be searched together. Saved in a notebook, the conversation sits next to your papers, videos and notes, where NotebookLM can cite it, compare it with other sources and turn it into a report or an Audio Overview.

Wikipedia

Retrieval-augmented generation

From Wikipedia, the free encyclopedia

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) is a technique that grants generative models the ability to retrieve and incorporate new information before responding.

Instead of relying only on what a model learned in training, RAG first searches a set of documents, then passes the most relevant passages to the model as context.

The approach reduces hallucinations and lets answers cite their sources, which is why research tools such as NotebookLM ground every answer in your uploads.

Bulk import to NotebookLM: dozens of sources in one run

NotebookTools adds many sources to one NotebookLM notebook in a single run. Add open tabs in the popup sends every website tab in the window in one click. On a YouTube playlist or channel, the popup lists its videos to tick. And right-clicking any page with Extract links to Notebook opens a queue of its links, where you can also paste more URLs. Each item shows its type and status, and failed items can be retried.

How to use it

  1. To add your open tabs, click the NotebookTools icon in the Chrome toolbar, pick a notebook and click Add open tabs. YouTube tabs go in as videos, the rest as web pages.
  2. For a YouTube playlist or channel, open its page and click the icon: tick the videos in the list and click Import N selected videos.
  3. For the links in an article or reading list, right-click the page and choose Extract links to Notebook. The queue opens with every link; paste more URLs (one per line) if you like.
  4. Untick anything you don't want, or select items and click Remove.
  5. Pick an existing notebook or create a new one.
  6. Click Import queue and watch the progress bar until the summary appears.

Why it helps

Adding sources by hand means one dialog per link, which is a lot of clicking for a reading list of 40 articles. The queue imports them in one go, skips links that are already in it, and warns you when it would push a notebook past its source limit (50 sources on a free Google account, 300 on Google AI Pro).

Manage sources: frequently asked questions

How do I delete multiple sources in NotebookLM?

Open the notebook in the NotebookTools hub, click Manage on the Sources tab, tick the sources and delete them together. The deletion applies to the notebook in NotebookLM.

How does the duplicate source scan work?

The fast scan groups sources with the same URL or title. The deep scan also compares the text of each source, so it finds copies saved under different names. You choose which copy to keep in each group.

Do source folders change my notebook in NotebookLM?

No. Folders only change how NotebookTools groups your sources and Studio files. Deleting a source does remove it from NotebookLM, so review before you confirm.

Prompt library: frequently asked questions

How do I use slash commands in NotebookLM?

Install NotebookTools, open a notebook and type / in the chat box. A menu lists your saved prompts and the slide templates; pick one with the arrow keys and Enter to insert it.

Can I save my own NotebookLM prompts?
